## Question

What is the resolution algorithm end to end: lookup order, when Google Places is called and what may be stored from it, normalization, scoring and thresholds, tiebreakers, and the fuzzy-search engine?

- **From "What a business entity is"** (2026-11-05, [ADR 0005](../../../docs/adr/0005-brands-companies-locations-separate.md)): a name in a quote must now resolve to a type as well as an entity ("Subway" may be the brand or the company; "Dunkin'" may be the brand or a location). Identifiers and addresses are sourced rows in their own tables; strong identifiers are unique; phones and addresses come only from stored documents, never Places; registered-agent addresses are never stored. Address normalization is this ticket's.
- **From "Schema conventions"** (2026-11-05; conventions in evidence_layer_plan.md §4; no on-demand research per decisions.md §15): resolution runs only on operator batches, so "Can a user disambiguate in A2?" is moot and the lookup order is for batches only. Whether search or the demand log calls Places is "Search and the demand log".
- **From "Claim producers and the claims door"** (2026-11-05; summary in evidence_layer_plan.md "Producers and the door"): the door calls the resolver in-process, inside its own transaction, once per identity record. The resolver matches or creates the entity and writes the sourced alias, identifier, address and industry rows, normalized for storage. The door has already checked each value against the document's text.
- **From "Predicate semantics"** (2026-11-05; role table in evidence_layer_plan.md §4 `claims`): candidates are filtered by the predicate's role table (the object of `operated_by` is always a company). When both a brand and a company fit ("Subway" in `acquired_by`, since a brand's owner may be named directly), the choice is this ticket's. Identity records now carry a location's brand names, which the resolver matches to brand entities for `location_brands` rows.
- **From chains-and-brands "Brand and chain model"** (2026-11-06): no location matching at launch (locations come with the store-map follow-up, operators with local). Brand-versus-company names matter: each consumer-facing name is a brand, a group of brands is a company, and Wendy's and The Wendy's Company are two rows. A brand's old name (Dunkin' Donuts) is a former-name alias that search must find.
- **From "Entity lifecycle"** (2026-11-06; notes in evidence_layer_plan.md §4 `entities`): an identity record whose strong identifiers belong to two same-type entities merges them; an identifier on one entity plus a name match on another is this ticket's rule to write, as are any other merge rules (name plus address) and a dedup pass that re-runs them over existing entities after a rules change. Superseded identity values stay as history rows; how much weight old values get in matching is this ticket's. The strong-identifier unique index skips retracted rows. Reads follow `merged_into_id` to the surviving entity.
- **From "Claim identity"** (2026-11-06; notes in evidence_layer_plan.md §4 `claims`): the door hands the resolver each claim's names as written (`subject_name`, `object_name`) plus the types the predicate's role table allows on that side. Outcomes: one clear match → id; no candidate → create a `company` (or a `family` when the LLM marked the side); ambiguous → null id, retried when identity data changes. Ids are the resolver's current answer: when this ticket's rules improve, code re-resolves claims and may change ids (events row, edge recompute).
- **From "Funds and their investment firm"** (2026-11-07; ADR 0001 amended; notes in evidence_layer_plan.md §4): one `investment_firm` entity is the firm as the public names it (KKR, L Catterton) and holds each of its adviser registrations' `crd` values (an entity may hold several values of one strong scheme). A listed parent (Blackstone Inc., KKR & Co. Inc., The Goldman Sachs Group, BlackRock, Inc.) is a separate company above the firm. When a bare name as written ("Blackstone", "KKR") matches both a firm and its listed parent, it should resolve to the firm; a 13G filer keyed by its CIK lands on the parent. How to do that is this ticket's.
- **From "Source independence and syndication"** (2026-11-08; ADR 0002 amended; notes in evidence_layer_plan.md §4 `documents`): a document's speaker is the entity holding its domain as an identifier (blackstone.com and bxpe.com are both Blackstone), so domain identifiers decide whose site a page is; matching domains to entities is this ticket's.
- **From "Press and EDGAR full-text connectors"** (2026-11-08; scrape etiquette in CLAUDE.md; press-connector note in evidence_layer_plan.md): a release's issuer (feed `dc:contributor` or the release's "SOURCE" line) must resolve to an entity, and the issuer's own site is found through its domain identifiers.
- **From "EDGAR full-text search and ownership filings"** (2026-11-08; section in evidence_layer_plan.md): 13D/13G reporting persons and EDGAR filers come with CIKs, a strong identifier, so they resolve by CIK first; a reporting person with no CIK resolves by name.
